What makes a Test bowler's average low?

An analysis of each bowler's first 100 Test wickets using Cricsheet ball-by-ball data. It is also a handbook for re-running and extending that analysis.

Window: first 100 Test wickets

The short answer

A low average comes mostly from taking wickets often, not from conceding few runs, and from getting batters out before they are set. Who the bowler is (pace or spin, arm, age at debut, batting hand) and where they played explain very little. Bowlers with low averages also tend to be the ones their captain gives the most overs, and they take more of their wickets in their second spell of a match.

    Evidence

    Strike rate does most of the work

    Average = strike rate × economy ÷ 6, so the log of the average is exactly the log of SR plus the log of economy (minus a constant). That lets the spread in averages across bowlers be split into an SR part and an economy part.

    Share of variance in log(average)AllPaceSpin

    Across all bowlers, strike rate carries about 70% of the variance and economy about 40%. The covariance term is slightly negative, so the shares add to roughly 100%. For spinners the two are about equal: a spinner's average depends on run control as much as on wicket-taking.

    Putting it together: a multivariate model

    A ridge regression of log(average) on 24 standardised non-mechanical features, with the penalty picked by leave-one-out cross-validation. Out-of-sample fit tells us how much of the average these attributes can really explain.

    Is a low average just favourable conditions?

    To test this, each bowler's average is compared with the match environment: runs per wicket conceded by every other bowler, on both sides, in the same matches. This one number captures pitch, era and opposition together.

    Handbook

    Data and scope

    • Source: Cricsheet men's JSON files in all_male_json/. Each file is one match with an info block (teams, venue, dates, match_type, registry.people name → ID) and an innings list of overs and deliveries.
    • Filter: info.match_type == "Test". The folder also holds ODIs, T20s and domestic matches. The old First 100 Wickets of Top Bowlers.csv mixed formats (only 850 of its 6,044 matches were Tests), so it is not used.
    • Population: bowlers with ≥ 100 bowler-credited Test wickets in the data (71). Window: every delivery from their first Test in the data up to and including the ball of their 100th wicket.
    • Left-censoring rule: Cricsheet's Test coverage is patchy before 2007 (as low as 36% of Tests in 2004). A bowler whose first Test in the data is before 2007-01-01 may have debuted earlier, or may have missing Tests inside the window. These 22 bowlers are left out of the main sample (49 remain) and added back in a robustness check.

    Pipeline

    Everything lives in Test Bowler Average Analysis.ipynb. Run it top to bottom from the project folder. The original notebook is untouched.

    1. Parse Tests to deliveries

      Loop over the JSON files, keep Tests, and write one row per delivery: IDs come from the registry; runs and extras by type; the bowler-credited wicket kind; player_out_id. Every dismissal also goes into a separate dismissals table, because one ball can carry two. Rows are sorted chronologically and given a global seq.

      out → df (1.72M rows), dismissals, matches
    2. Batting position

      The order in which a player first appears at the crease, as striker or non-striker, in each innings. This is the original notebook's logic, keyed on player ID.

    3. Select bowlers and cut at the 100th wicket 
      SUM(is_wicket) OVER (PARTITION BY bowler_id ORDER BY seq
                     ROWS UNBOUNDED PRECEDING) AS wickets_taken
...
WHERE wickets_taken - is_wicket < 100   -- keeps the 100th-wicket ball, nothing after

      This fixes three problems in the original: the count was partitioned by name, run-outs were counted when selecting bowlers, and formats were mixed. A check asserts exactly 100 wickets per bowler.

      out → Test First 100 Wickets.csv (same columns as before + match_type)
    4. Attach player metadata

      Join player_styles.csv (scraped by scrape_player_styles.py) for bowling style, batting hand and date of birth. The original parse_bowling_style, parse_batting_hand and batting_position_label are reused. All 71 bowlers matched.

    5. Dismissed-batter context

      For every batter, compute career totals (runs, balls, dismissals, innings, Tests) entering each match using all Test deliveries. Join them to each wicket, along with the batter's runs and balls in that innings at the moment of dismissal.

      out → test_wicket_context.csv (one row per wicket)
    6. Bowler features

      Aggregate each window to one row per bowler: the target, mechanical stats, wicket mix, discipline, workload and context. The conditions measures always exclude the bowler's own deliveries.

      out → test_bowler_features.csv (71 × 58)
    7. Analysis and export

      Decomposition, univariate correlations, partial correlations, tertile profile, ridge and random-forest models, conditions adjustment, robustness. Results are written to report_results.json, which feeds this page.

    Definitions

    Bowler wicket
    Dismissal kind in caught, bowled, lbw, caught and bowled, stumped, hit wicket. Run outs, retirements and obstruction don't count.
    Legal ball
    A delivery that is neither a wide nor a no-ball.
    Bowler runs
    runs_total − byes − legbyes − penalty. Wides and no-balls are charged to the bowler.
    Average
    Bowler runs ÷ 100 wickets.
    Strike rate
    Legal balls ÷ wickets.
    Economy
    Bowler runs per 6 legal balls. Identity: average = SR × economy ÷ 6, which the notebook asserts.
    Bowler innings
    Within a match, the dense rank of the innings in which the bowler bowled: 1 = their first bowling innings, 2 = their second. This is not the match innings number 1–4.
    Batting band
    Top order 1–3, middle 4–7, tail 8–11.
    Dismissed batter career avg
    The batter's Test average entering the match, shrunk toward their band's typical average using 5 pseudo-dismissals: (runs + 5 × band_avg) ÷ (outs + 5). This stops debutants and short careers getting extreme values.
    Match environment
    Runs per wicket conceded by all other bowlers, on both teams, in the bowler's window matches.
    Venue environment
    Long-run runs per wicket at each venue, excluding the bowler, weighted by the balls they bowled there.
    Opposition batting
    Runs per wicket each batting team scored that calendar year against all other bowlers, weighted by balls bowled to them.
    Team ball share
    The bowler's legal balls ÷ their team's legal balls in the same matches.
    Relative average
    Average ÷ match environment.

    Statistical method

    • Small n (49). Every estimate carries a 95% bootstrap interval (2,000 resamples). Treat anything under |ρ| ≈ 0.3 as weak.
    • Spearman, not Pearson. It uses ranks, so it is robust to outliers such as Philander's 17.4 and to non-linear but monotonic relationships.
    • Benjamini–Hochberg FDR. 44 features are tested, so about 2 would look "significant" at p < 0.05 by chance. q-values control the expected share of false discoveries, and q < 0.10 counts as a finding.
    • Mechanical vs explanatory. SR, economy, matches-to-100, overs-to-100, dot % and maidens are arithmetic parts of the average. Including them in an attribution model would "explain" everything and teach nothing. They are analysed separately in the decomposition instead.
    • Outcome-adjacent features (the dismissed batter's runs, balls and SR in that innings) are partly made of the bowler's own runs conceded. They are reported, but kept out of Model A.
    • Ridge regression on standardised features handles correlated predictors with few observations. The penalty is chosen by leave-one-out CV, coefficients are bootstrapped 1,000 times at that penalty, and the target is log(average), so coefficients read as % change.
    • Random forest with permutation importance (5-fold × 10 repeats) checks for non-linear effects the ridge would miss. It found none.
    • Partial Spearman. All variables are ranked, the control is regressed out of both sides, and the residuals are correlated.

    Reading the charts

    • Forest plots: a dot is the estimate and the line is its 95% interval. If the line crosses zero, the direction is not established.
    • Iso-average scatter: curves join every SR/economy pair giving the same average. Moving along a curve trades wicket-taking for run control.

    Re-run and extend

    Run it

    conda activate data_work          # pandas 3, duckdb, scikit-learn, scipy, seaborn
cd "Cricket Bowler Average Attribution"
jupyter lab "Test Bowler Average Analysis.ipynb"   # Run All (≈ 2–3 minutes)

    Change the window or the population

    Edit the constants in the setup cell: WICKET_WINDOW (e.g. 150), MIN_CAREER_WICKETS (keep it ≥ the window) and FULL_COVERAGE_FROM. Everything downstream reads them.

    Add a feature

    1. Compute it per bowler in section 4 as a column on feat, using the window table w or wickets.
    2. Register it in the FEATURES dict: 'H20': ('col', 'Label', 'group'). Use group mechanical or outcome-adjacent if it is arithmetically linked to runs or wickets.
    3. If it is a genuine attribute, add it to MODEL_FEATURES. All tables, the forest plot and the export pick it up automatically.

    New Cricsheet data

    Replace all_male_json/ with a fresh download and re-run. If new bowlers need styles, add their rows to scrape_targets.csv and re-run scrape_player_styles.py, which skips players it already has. The notebook prints any bowler missing a style.

    Caveats

    • Correlation, not causation. The workload-share and team-win signals almost certainly run partly backwards: captains bowl, and teams win with, bowlers who are already succeeding.
    • Small sample. 49 bowlers in the main analysis, with only 17 spinners. Spin-only results are suggestive at best.
    • Coverage. Cricsheet is missing some Tests, especially before 2007, plus a few later ones (about 92–98% coverage in some years). Averages can differ slightly from official figures. Batter "experience" is undercounted for players whose careers began before 2001.
    • Home/away excluded. Matches can't be reliably marked home or away (neutral venues, and a venue's country isn't in the data), so this hypothesis was not tested.
    • Bowling-style labels come from Cricinfo's first listed style. A bowler who changed style is classed by that first entry.
